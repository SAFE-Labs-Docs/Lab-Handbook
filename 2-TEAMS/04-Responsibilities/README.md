# I commit to publicly document the responsibilities of each lab role and the training provided

This page collects real-world examples of lab role responsibilities from labs around the world. We encourage all labs implementing the SAFE Labs Handbook to share their own approach here. To contribute, open a pull request that adds your example to this README under `## Examples`, below the `###` heading for your country (add the heading if it is missing, naming the country as the map does, such as United States or United Kingdom). Write the example as `>_[LabName_YEAR](URL)_: text`, quoting your text exactly and starting every further line of it with `>`, blank lines included, and leave a blank line after the country heading and between examples. The link is optional, but only linked examples appear on the safelabs.info map.

## Details
Each group leader has their own expectations for lab roles (PhD students, postdoctoral researchers, lab technicians etc.). By explicitly stating the responsibilities associated to different positions, group leaders can pre-empt expectation mismatch for prospective lab members before and after they join the lab.

## Suggestions
- What is the training/experience/independence expected for each role?
- Does the role involve a research project?
- Does the role involve applying for funding?
- Does the role involve supervising other lab members?
- Will the role be supervised by another lab member?
- Does the role involve house-keeping tasks?
- What level of supervision/training will be offered?

## Examples

### Australia
>_[BABLab_2026](https://github.com/BaBLab-UNSW/Lab_Manual/blob/main/02-expectations-and-responsibilities.md)_:
>
>**Lab Managers**
>
>- Collect data for the ongoing group projects in the lab.
>- Manage the lab's data backup and ensure backups happen on a consistent, documented schedule (see Section on Data Access & Management).
>- Optional: work on your own research (developed with Bridget's help) — a review paper, analysis of an existing dataset, a study within the group project, or another project outside it.
>- Create research and lab protocols.
>- Train new lab members on research and lab protocols.
>- Monitor adherence to research and lab protocols.
>- Hire, train, mentor, and assess progress of undergraduate research assistants, including setting them up with lab access, making sure they're properly onboarded, and making sure they're added to the ethics approvals for the projects they're working on.
>- Schedule and manage the work of undergraduate research assistants.
>- Maintain human research ethics applications for the lab (writing them, renewing them via the annual/periodic reporting UNSW's HREAP process requires), archive old consent forms, and keep required paperwork up to date and organised.
>- Maintain the lab website and wiki, update the lab manual, add lab events to shared calendars, manage the lab's shared drive, and check and respond to the lab's shared email/inbox.
>- Give new lab members access to the wiki, GitHub, shared calendars, and add their experiments to the shared drive.

>_[SMCCLab_2026](https://smcclab.github.io/honsproj/)_:
>
>As a student you can expect the following:
>
>- individual meetings when needed (but not every week)
>- lab meetings to show what you have done and hear about others' work
>- access to important information and materials (but not necessarily huge GPUs)
>- feedback on your project plan and implementation
>- feedback on thesis/report drafts in **5 business days**
>- questions answered in **1 business day** (during work hours only)
>- realistic feedback about quality of written work and software/hardware artefacts
>- help and kindness when things go wrong
>
>As a supervisor I expect:
>
>- students to show up to scheduled meetings
>- students to take planning seriously
>- students to help each other and share information
>- draft thesis/reports to be sent 2 weeks before any deadline
>- patience when asking questions or sending emails out of business hours

### Brazil
>_[CompGeoLab_2026](https://www.compgeolab.org/manual/expectations.html)_:
>
>**PhD students**
>
>- **Be independent** in coming up with your research topics and ideas for what to do next. Many of these will be tied to the lab's ongoing projects but this is the time to start thinking for yourself.

>- **Study broadly**, beyond your research topic. A PhD is a wonderful time to explore different topics, research fields, career pathways, and ideas. >

>- **Help in teaching and outreach** efforts of the lab. This does not mean teaching a full course or replacing Leo in any capacity. It means providing a lecture or two about your expertise in a course, leading a short workshop, helping Leo organize outreach, etc. All of these are very good things to have on a CV when applying for an academic job.

### China

>_[SHUDGroup_2026](https://www.shud.xyz/book_lab/)_:
>
>科研团队负责人（Principal Investigator, PI）的核心职责，是让团队在正确方向上，持续产出可复现、可发表、可积累的成果：
>
>- 定方向与标准：明确研究问题、技术路线与“什么算完成/算好”（可复现、可解释、可推广），定期收敛与调整选题。
>- 建梯队与分工：组建互补的人员结构（博士/硕士/助理/联培等），明确分工接口与协作规则，保证关键任务有人负责、有人备份。
>- 资源与平台保障：争取并配置经费、数据、设备、算力与外部资源；把有限资源投向能形成长期积累的方向与基础设施。
>- 质量把关：对数据、代码、方法、统计与结论负责；推动代码与数据可追溯、关键结果可复现；把科研诚信与合规作为底线。
>- 学术产出与传播：把项目推进为论文/报告/开源/专利等可交付成果，抓关键节点（投稿、返修、答辩、结题），并维护团队学术声誉。
>- 培养与评估：设定训练路径与里程碑（文献、方法、写作、表达），提供高频反馈；基于贡献与成长进行公正评价与机会分配。
>- 文化与风险管理：建立透明沟通、互相尊重、可预期的制度；及时处理冲突、倦怠与安全问题，降低单点故障与项目风险。
>
>PI对方向、资源与质量负责，最终以可复现成果与团队成长为交付。
>
>1.2.2 科研助理
>
>科研助理的核心职责，是把团队的研究工作“落到地上”，让项目按计划推进并保持可追溯：
>
>- 执行支撑任务：按要求完成文献检索与整理、数据收集/清洗、实验/模拟的批量运行、结果可视化与表格汇总。
>- 材料与流程管理：维护项目文档、数据与代码的目录结构与版本记录；整理会议纪要、任务清单与阶段交付物。
>- 质量与合规：按规范记录参数、环境与关键步骤；对数据来源、引用与共享权限负责，避免“口算结果/不可复现”。
>- 协调与保障：协助安排组会与学术活动、差旅/报销/采购等事务性流程，让研究成员把时间花在关键科研上。
>
>科研助理以规范执行与流程保障提升团队效率，确保材料与结果可追溯。
>
>_English translation:_
>
>The core duty of a research team leader (Principal Investigator, PI) is to make the team keep producing reproducible, publishable and cumulative results in the right direction:
>
>- Set direction and standards: define the research questions, the technical route and what counts as done or good (reproducible, explainable, generalisable), and regularly narrow and adjust the choice of topics.
>- Build the team and divide the work: put together a complementary mix of people (PhD students, master's students, assistants, jointly trained students and so on), define the interfaces between tasks and the rules of collaboration, and make sure every key task has someone responsible and someone as backup.
>- Secure resources and platforms: win and allocate funding, data, equipment, computing power and external resources, and direct the limited resources to directions and infrastructure that can build up over the long term.
>- Quality control: be responsible for the data, code, methods, statistics and conclusions; push for traceable code and data and reproducible key results; treat research integrity and compliance as the bottom line.
>- Academic output and dissemination: carry projects through to deliverables such as papers, reports, open source and patents, watch the key milestones (submission, revision, defence, project closure), and protect the academic reputation of the team.
>- Training and assessment: set training paths and milestones (literature, methods, writing, presentation), give frequent feedback, and evaluate fairly and allocate opportunities on the basis of contribution and growth.
>- Culture and risk management: build transparent communication, mutual respect and predictable rules; deal promptly with conflict, burnout and safety problems, and reduce single points of failure and project risk.
>
>The PI is responsible for direction, resources and quality, and in the end delivers reproducible results and the growth of the team.
>
>1.2.2 Research assistants
>
>The core duty of a research assistant is to bring the team's research work down to earth, so that projects move forward as planned and stay traceable:
>
>- Carry out supporting tasks: complete literature searches and organisation, data collection and cleaning, batch runs of experiments and simulations, result visualisation and table summaries as required.
>- Manage materials and processes: maintain the directory structure and version records of project documents, data and code; organise meeting minutes, task lists and stage deliverables.
>- Quality and compliance: record parameters, environment and key steps according to the standard; be responsible for data sources, citations and sharing permissions, and avoid "results calculated in the head" that cannot be reproduced.
>- Coordination and support: help arrange group meetings and academic activities, and handle administrative processes such as travel, reimbursement and purchasing, so that research members can spend their time on the key research.
>
>Research assistants raise team efficiency through standardised execution and process support, and make sure that materials and results are traceable.
